## Over-the-Air Computation

Over-the-Air Computation (AirComp) is an emerging paradigm that seamlessly integrates communication and computation by harnessing the superposition property of wireless multi-access channels to compute target functions directly over the air. By simultaneously aggregating distributed signals, AirComp drastically reduces latency and bandwidth consumption, making it a key enabler for massive data aggregation in next-generation networks. Our group focuses on advancing AirComp across three key frontiers: (1) Performance Optimization, including joint transceiver power control, digital modulation, and channel coding; (2) Application
Exploration, with an emphasis on federated edge learning, distributed consensus, and massive IoT sensing; and (3) Secure and Private Transmission, developing physical-layer security techniques and privacy-preserving mechanisms against eavesdropping, jamming, and inference attacks.

## Embodied Intelligent Wireless Communication

Embodied Intelligent Wireless Communication lies at the intersection of embodied intelligence and next-generation wireless networks, focusing on how wireless connectivity can enable autonomous agents to perceive, communicate, coordinate, and act in complex physical environments. Going beyond conventional communication paradigms, our research investigates wireless systems in which the channel, network, and physical behavior of intelligent agents are tightly coupled. Our group explores three key directions: (1) Fast Channel Intelligence for Mobile Agents, developing rapid channel estimation and prediction techniques to support reliable communication among highly dynamic and spatially distributed embodied agents; (2) Robust Multi-Agent Networking and Anti-Interference Communication, studying resilient network formation, dynamic topology adaptation, and interference-resistant transmission for large-scale collaborative agents; and (3) Embodied Communication under Extreme Wireless Conditions, jointly designing trajectory planning, agent mobility, and semantic information transmission to maintain effective coordination and ensure the reliable delivery of task-critical information even under severe channel impairments.

## Wireless Brain-Computer Interface Communication

Wireless Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) Communication represents an emerging frontier at the intersection of wireless communications, neuroengineering, and computational neuroscience, aiming to establish high-throughput, energy-efficient, and reliable wireless links between the human nervous system and external cyber-physical systems. By replacing physical connections with wireless interfaces, BCI systems introduce unique challenges arising from stringent constraints on energy consumption, tissue safety, wireless channel dynamics, and neural information fidelity. Our group investigates wireless BCI from three complementary perspectives: (1) Ultra-Low-Power Wireless Neural Communication, developing energy-efficient transceivers, wireless power and data transfer, and neural signal compression techniques to support high-density implantable interfaces under stringent energy and thermal constraints; (2) Neural Channel Intelligence and Reliable Implant Networking, studying in-vivo wireless channel modeling, channel estimation, interference management, and distributed networking to enable robust communication among densely deployed neural implants and external devices; and (3) Semantic, Secure, and Ultra-Reliable BCI Communication, developing task-oriented neural communication, importance-aware transmission, and physical-layer security mechanisms to ensure the reliable and secure delivery of critical neural information for real-time closed-loop neuroprosthetic systems.
