## Over-the-Air Computation

Over-the-Air Computation (AirComp) is an emerging paradigm that seamlessly integrates communication and computation by harnessing the superposition property of wireless multi-access channels to compute target functions directly over the air. By simultaneously aggregating distributed signals, AirComp drastically reduces latency and bandwidth consumption, making it a key enabler for massive data aggregation in next-generation networks. Our laboratory focuses on advancing AirComp across three key frontiers: (1) Performance Optimization, including joint transceiver beamforming, channel synchronization, and integration with reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS); (2) Application
Exploration, with an emphasis on federated edge learning, distributed consensus, and massive IoT sensing; and (3) Secure and Private Transmission, developing physical-layer security techniques and privacy-preserving mechanisms against eavesdropping, jamming, and inference attacks.

## Embodied Intelligent Wireless Communication

Embodied Intelligent Wireless Communication is an emerging frontier at the intersection of embodied artificial intelligence and next-generation wireless networks. Moving beyond traditional disembodied AI, it establishes a tightly coupled, closed-loop paradigm integrating perception, communication, computation, decision-making, and physical actuation, where autonomous agents (e.g., robotic platforms, UAVs, and autonomous vehicles) actively interact with dynamic physical and electromagnetic environments. Our laboratory explores this domain across three key pillars: (1) Task-Oriented Perception-Control-Communication Co-Design, developing multimodal semantic communication and ultra-reliable, low-latency protocols tailored for real-time robotic telemetry and physical action; (2) Multi-Agent Collaborative Embodied Intelligence, investigating distributed reinforcement learning, swarm navigation, and physical-cyber resource orchestration under constrained wireless connectivity; and (3) Proactive Network Adaptation via Mobile Embodiment, leveraging mobility-enabled robotic transceivers and active metasurfaces to physically explore, navigate, and dynamically reshape the wireless propagation environment.

## Wireless Brain-Computer Interface Communication

Wireless Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) Communication represents a transformative frontier at the convergence of wireless networking, neuroengineering, and computational neuroscience. By eliminating physical percutaneous wires, it establishes high-throughput, untethered, and bidirectional communication channels between the human nervous system and external cyber-physical systems. Our laboratory advances wireless BCI systems across three fundamental pillars: (1) Ultra-Low-Power & Thermally Safe In-Vivo Telemetry, designing energy-harvesting transceivers (e.g., RF backscatter, near-field inductive coupling, and ultrasound/optical links) and semantic neural compression algorithms to support massive-channel microelectrode arrays under stringent in-vivo heat dissipation limits; (2) Bidirectional Neural Decoding & Closed-Loop Neuromodulation, developing brain-inspired AI decoders for real-time motor and speech intent extraction (brain-to-device) alongside precision electrical/optical stimulus encoding for sensory feedback restoration (device-to-brain); and (3) Deterministic Brain-in-the-Loop Networking & Neuro-Security, establishing ultra-reliable low-latency protocols for real-time neuro-prosthetic control, distributed implantable nano-networks, and physical-layer privacy frameworks to prevent neural data eavesdropping and adversarial neuro-interference.
